Method & limits

How the shortfall is calculated

The bound nobody can argue with

21 CFR 101.36 lets a manufacturer hide the dose of every ingredient inside a proprietary blend — but it requires those ingredients to be listed in descending order by weight, and it requires the blend's total weight on the label.

Those two rules together produce a hard ceiling. If an ingredient is listed kth, every ingredient above it weighs at least as much, so k of them together cannot exceed the blend total. Therefore that ingredient is at most total ÷ k.

When total ÷ k lands below the researched effective dose, the shortfall is not an estimate or an accusation — it is arithmetic that follows from the label's own declared numbers.

Why every vial fills to the same line

A vial's fill is the fraction of its own effective dose that is present, not an absolute weight. Creatine at 5 g and zinc at 20 mg are three orders of magnitude apart, so plotting raw mass would make everything but the largest ingredient invisible. Filling each to its own target puts the ring at one height across the row, which is what makes a gap readable at a glance.

The mass check

Separately, the panel adds up one effective dose of every active and compares that total to the declared weight and to the serving size. A formula whose effective version outweighs its own scoop cannot be dosed properly at that serving, whatever the label says.

What this is not

Educational tool, not medical, nutritional or regulatory advice, and not a compliance review. Dose ranges are commonly cited effective ranges from human trials in healthy adults, summarised for teaching — they are not personalised, they take no account of body mass, stacking, tolerance, or any medical condition, and reputable sources disagree at the margins. "Provably short" describes only the arithmetic ceiling created by the descending-order rule; it is a statement about what a declared blend weight can physically contain, never an allegation about any manufacturer. All example products are invented. Nothing you do here leaves your browser. Speak to a doctor or a registered dietitian before changing what you take.
